Australia visa for Indians

Indians need a Visitor visa (subclass 600, Tourist stream) for Australia. The application is fully online, and biometrics are needed only if Home Affairs asks.

Visa typeVisitor (subclass 600)
StayUsually 3 months per visit
Government feeAUD 250 (≈ ₹16,000)
ProcessingUsually 3–6 weeks

Information checked: 23 October 2026. Rules and fees change often; we confirm the latest before you apply.

Other fees

  • VFS biometrics charge if requested.
  • Medical tests, paid to the panel clinic, for longer stays or applicants aged 75+.

Processing time: Half of applications are decided within about a month and most within 2 months; October to February is slower.

Documents checklist

  • Passport valid 6+ months, all stamped pages and old passports
  • Recent passport-style photo
  • PAN card and Aadhaar
  • Bank statements (6 months) and salary slips
  • ITRs or Form 16 (2–3 years)
  • Employer leave letter, or GST and business papers
  • Day-wise itinerary, flight reservation and hotel bookings
  • Invitation letter and host's status proof if visiting family
  • Cover letter explaining the trip and your ties to India

How we apply for you

  1. Send your passport front and back pages and travel dates on WhatsApp.
  2. We confirm the right visa and send a personalised checklist.
  3. We fill the application and review every document before submission.
  4. We track the application and send you the decision as soon as it is issued.

Our service fee is listed on the Pricing page. Government fees are charged at actuals and are not refundable if a visa is refused.

Before you fly

  • The fee rose from AUD 200 on 1 July 2026 and is reviewed every July.
  • The fee is not refunded if the visa is refused.
  • Each family member pays separately, though applications can be grouped.
  • Health insurance is strongly recommended.
  • Book non-refundable travel only after the visa is granted.

Official sources

  • Australian Home Affairs: Visitor visa (subclass 600)
  • Australian High Commission, India

This page is general guidance, not legal advice. The final decision on any visa rests with the issuing government.
